Overview

The Challenge

E-commerce websites often struggle to provide accessible navigation and intuitive search tools, especially for older users. The Animal Farmacy homepage suffered from a lack of search functionality, poorly structured product categories, and a lack of visible, user-friendly navigation options.

These problems created frustration among users — especially older farmers with limited digital knowledge — and prevented fast and efficient product discovery.

This project focused on redesigning the homepage to prioritise accessibility, intuitive navigation, and user-centric search functionality.

💡

Problem Statement

How might we make the search and navigation tools more intuitive and visible, so that older users — particularly farmers — can easily and confidently find specific products without frustration or confusion?

Background

Context & User Base

This case study is based on a real-world project where the client requested that I improve the user experience of their e-commerce platform. With an average user demographic of farmers aged 57+, the challenges included:

  • Difficulty in locating specific products due to unclear navigation
  • Ineffective use of search functionality tools, resulting in user frustration
  • Poor alignment with accessibility standards, limiting usability for older users

Design Process: Double Diamond

I chose the Double Diamond framework because it provides a clear, structured approach to problem-solving — first exploring user challenges widely, then narrowing down on specific, actionable solutions. Its iterative nature ensured a thorough understanding of user needs through discovery and definition phases, followed by targeted design and delivery phases.

01
Phase One
Research & Findings
Primary Research

How I Understood the Problem

Heuristic Evaluation

Identified key usability issues including unclear navigation, mismatched visuals, cluttered layout, and a lack of customer support options.

Competitor Analysis

Analysed best practices from similar e-commerce platforms in Ireland and globally (Valley Vet, Farmers Market, Next, Chupi).

User Interviews

Conducted with farmers and farming-related users aged 25–65+ to understand their online shopping behaviour and pain points.

Accessibility Audit

Evaluated the site against WCAG standards for contrast, readability, and navigation accessibility.

Key Findings

Navigation Issues

  • Hidden menus and unclear category names confused users
  • Older users struggled to recognise the magnifying glass as a search icon
  • Breadcrumb navigation was missing, leaving users uncertain of their location

"If someone doesn't see the menu they can always use the search bar" — user

Search Bar Challenges

  • The search bar was small, lacked contrast, and was not intuitive
  • The magnifying glass icon was not recognised by 40% of older users
  • Users relied on scrolling or trial-and-error instead of using the search bar

Information Architecture Problems

  • Categories like "Farm Equipment" and "Health Products" were too broad
  • Products were not grouped logically (e.g. "Calf Jackets" misplaced)
  • Shipping and contact information were not visible and difficult to find

"I would put calf jacket under the farm equipment" — user

Accessibility

  • Users complained about colours, text size (too small), and buttons that are too small
  • Hidden hamburger menu was unfamiliar to older users
  • No descriptive text to help users take the correct action

Synthesis

Turning Research into Insight

Empathy Map

After collecting data from heuristic evaluations, interviews, case studies, usability tests, and surveys, I incorporated user feedback into an empathy map to better understand their frustrations, goals, and behaviours.

🧠 Think

Users were confused about navigation and felt unsure where to start their search.

💭 Feel

Frustrated, overwhelmed, and disoriented by poorly labelled menus and hidden search functionality.

🗣 Say

"Where do I even begin?" — "The search bar is too hard to find."

🖱 Do

Relied on trial-and-error methods like scrolling endlessly or guessing categories.

Personas

After creating three personas with their pain points, goals, frustrations, and journey maps, I focused on one: Jack.

👨‍🌾
Jack
Irish Farmer, 62 — Limited digital experience

Why Jack?
Jack represents the group of older farmers (average age of 57 in Ireland), users who would love to buy online but lack tech literacy. Fixing the problem for older users like Jack will have a positive impact and fix problems for all other users — accessibility features benefit everyone.

  • Struggles to recognise icons and navigate dropdown menus
  • Frustrated by insufficient product descriptions
  • Needs to shop while on the go — in barns, fields, outdoors
  • A returning customer if the experience is easy and trustworthy

How Might We?

Since issues with product search and navigation from the homepage kept repeating across all personas, I was able to narrow them down into a single problem statement:

💡

Problem Statement

How might we make the search and navigation tools more intuitive and visible, so that older users — particularly farmers — can easily and confidently find specific products without frustration or confusion?

From Insights to Ideas

Challenge

Old Homepage vs. Redesigned Homepage

Mood Board & Accessibility

To ensure my search tools met accessibility standards, I researched examples of effective search boxes — including those with written instructions within the field. I also verified that colour ratios met accessibility standards while representing Animal Farmacy's brand.

Menu Design

My previous usability testing showed users expected certain categories on the homepage itself — not hidden in a menu. Shipping information, FAQ, contact details, and product categories all needed to be visible. This led me to identify 4 main categories for a visible, always-present navigation menu.

Paper Prototypes

After creating the mood board with good examples of search tools, accessible web pages, better colour ratios, and a more organised menu, I created four paper prototypes to quickly test with real users before moving to digital.

Guerilla Testing

Testing in the Real World

To communicate directly with users, I took my paper prototypes and conducted Guerilla testing in my workplace, where I was able to find older users — including some ex-farmers. By mixing the order of papers and questions, I gathered more precise results with fewer biases.

Questions Asked

  1. How would you start your search to find farm supplies on this homepage?
  2. Where would you expect to find blogs or articles?
  3. Show me with your finger where you would start your search for animal medicine.
  4. Where would you look for company contact details?
  5. If you could point with your finger, where would you start if searching for minerals or supplements?
  6. What part of the homepage draws your attention first just by looking at it?
💡

Key Insight

One paper prototype stood out by its ease of finding the search bar — this design principle carried directly into the final digital prototype.

The Digital Prototype

I created a high-fidelity prototype in Figma incorporating all key learnings from research, testing, and stakeholder feedback.

🎨 View Figma Prototype →

Key Features of the Redesign

  • Improved Search Functionality — Prominently positioned with descriptive placeholder text ("Type your text here") and example text below. Enlarged for better visibility.
  • Logical Navigation Structure — Visible menu with regrouped main categories for easier navigation.
  • Accessibility First — Font sizes increased to 18pt inside the search box and 40pt for headings. High-contrast colours meet WCAG ratio standards.
  • Shipping Information on Homepage — Visible at the top of the page so users don't have to search for it.
  • Instructional Text in Search Box — Helps users know what to do next without guessing.

Digital Prototype Testing (Maze)

For the digital prototype, I used Maze to create tasks and questions testing visibility and use of the search tools. The link was sent to 6 users aged 35–55+. All users completed all tasks and answered all questions.

Conclusion

Reflecting on the Work

Working on the Animal Farmacy homepage, I discovered many challenges in search functionality, navigation, and accessibility. By focusing on user feedback — interviews, surveys, usability testing — and meeting accessibility standards, I created a new design that helps users locate search tools more easily and navigate the platform with more confidence.

These changes are now a strong base for future improvements like AI-powered search and personalised recommendations, keeping the platform both user-friendly, competitive, and business-successful.

Learnings

What I Took Away

Working on this project, I learned a lot. It was challenging to focus on just one thing to improve — but that constraint was the biggest teacher.

It's ok to ask for help when stuck. You don't have to know everything.
When in doubt, go back to your research. It always has the answer.
Every design decision needs to be backed by research — not intuition alone.
Empathy-driven research reveals surprising pain points you never would have imagined.
Keep the design simple and accessible — design for everybody.
Step away from technology sometimes. Paper prototypes and Guerilla testing reveal things digital tools can't.
The best aha moments often happen away from the screen — walking, observing, chatting with people.
Write everything down — your thoughts, ideas, instructions for users. Always write.
Always think outside the box. The most obvious solution is rarely the best one.
