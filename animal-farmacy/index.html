The Project

Why This Redesign?

E-commerce websites often struggle to provide accessible navigation and intuitive search tools, especially for older users. The Animal Farmacy homepage suffered from a lack of search functionality, poorly structured product categories, and navigation options that were simply invisible to its core audience — farmers aged 57 and above.

This project focused on redesigning the homepage to prioritise accessibility, intuitive navigation, and user-centric search functionality.

Understanding the Context

Design Process — Double Diamond

I chose the Double Diamond framework because it provides a clear, structured approach to problem-solving — first exploring user challenges widely, then narrowing down on specific, actionable solutions. With an average user demographic of farmers aged 57+, the challenges included:

  • Difficulty locating specific products due to unclear navigation
  • Ineffective use of search functionality, resulting in user frustration
  • Poor alignment with accessibility standards, limiting usability for older users

Phase One
Primary Research
Research Methods

How I Understood the Problem

Heuristic Evaluation

Identified key usability issues: unclear navigation, mismatched visuals, cluttered layout, and lack of customer support.

Competitor Analysis

Analysed best practices from platforms in Ireland and globally: Valley Vet, Farmers Market, Next, Chupi.

User Interviews

Conducted with farmers and farming-related users aged 25–65+ to understand their online shopping behaviour.

Usability Testing

Focused on key tasks: finding specific products using search tools, and locating shipping information.

What the Research Revealed

Navigation Issues

  • Hidden menus and unclear category names confused users
  • Older users struggled to recognise the magnifying glass as a search icon
  • Breadcrumb navigation was missing — users were lost

"If someone doesn't see the menu, they can always use the search bar." — user

Search Bar Challenges

  • The search bar was small, lacked contrast, and was not intuitive
  • 40% of older users could not recognise the magnifying glass icon
  • Users relied on scrolling and trial-and-error instead of searching

Information Architecture Problems

  • Categories like "Farm Equipment" were too broad
  • Products not grouped logically — "Calf Jackets" was misplaced
  • Shipping and contact information were hidden and hard to find

"I would put calf jacket under the farm equipment." — user

Accessibility

  • Users complained about colours, small text sizes, and buttons too small to tap
  • The hidden hamburger menu was unfamiliar to older users
  • No descriptive text to guide users towards correct actions

Phase Two
Empathy Map & Personas
Define

Understanding Our Users

Empathy Map

After collecting data from heuristic evaluations, interviews, usability tests, and surveys, I built an empathy map to better understand user frustrations, goals, and behaviours. This helped highlight the main challenges users were facing.

🧠 Think

Confused about navigation, unsure where to start their search.

💭 Feel

Frustrated, overwhelmed, disoriented by poorly labelled menus.

🗣 Say

"Where do I even begin?" — "The search bar is too hard to find."

🖱 Do

Scroll endlessly and guess at categories instead of using the search bar.

Personas

After creating three personas with their pain points, goals, frustrations, and journey maps, I focused on one: Jack. Jack represents older Irish farmers (average age 57) who would love to buy online but lack tech literacy. Fixing the problem for Jack also fixes it for everyone — accessibility features benefit all users.

How Might We?

Since issues with product search and navigation from the homepage kept repeating across all personas, I was able to narrow them down into a single problem statement.

💡 Problem Statement

How might we make the search and navigation tools more intuitive and visible, so that older users — particularly farmers — can easily and confidently find specific products without frustration or confusion?

Mood Board, Menu & Paper Prototypes

Building the Design Direction

Mood Board — Search Accessibility

To ensure my search tools met accessibility standards, I researched effective search box examples — including those with written instructions inside the field. I also verified that colour ratios met WCAG standards while representing Animal Farmacy's brand.

Mood board — competitor examples of accessible search bars (Rightmove, Next)

WCAG contrast checker — original site failing all standards at 1.98:1

Menu restructure — information architecture with 4 main categories

Paper Prototypes

After creating the mood board with examples of search tools, accessible web pages, better colour ratios, and a more organised menu, I created four paper prototypes. By mixing the order of papers and questions, I gathered more precise results with fewer biases.

Guerilla Testing

I took my paper prototypes to my workplace and tested them with older users — including some ex-farmers — asking them to find products, contact details, blogs, and the search bar.

💡 Key Finding

One paper prototype stood out clearly by its ease of finding the search bar. This design carried directly into the final digital prototype.

Conclusion

What This Project Taught Me

Working on the Animal Farmacy homepage, I discovered many challenges in search functionality, navigation, and accessibility. By focusing on user feedback and meeting accessibility standards, I created a new design that helps users locate search tools more easily and navigate the platform with confidence.

Design for the extremes — if it works for a 70-year-old farmer in a barn, it works for everyone.
Search is primary — it's not a nice-to-have, it's the core journey for older users.
Information architecture shapes behaviour — poor categories confuse even motivated users.
Test with real users. Guerilla testing revealed surprises no desk research could predict.
When in doubt, go back to the research. It's the compass throughout the project.
Paper before pixels. Old-fashioned prototypes revealed what no digital tool could.
